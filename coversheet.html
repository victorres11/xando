Victor Torres - A little background

My original passion was working in competitive sports. After a (unsuccessful) walk-on tryout at Arizona State, I worked on the recruiting department at ASU - cutting tapes of high school prospects to be passed on to the coaches. Unfortunately, within a few months - there was a coaching staff overhaul and I was let go. As they say - First in, first out. Somewhat regretfully, as an inexperienced 18 year old I let this experience jade me in terms of the industry and thought that since I didn't have a substantial network in the sport - I had a low chance of being able to find a real opportunity to make that my career.

Through a series of random events I then decided to focus my collegiate studies on Business - and post college found my way working in technology companies. That was never the plan, but it worked out for me. I cut my teeth at a tech company with an entry level customer service job, became enamored with software engineering, taught myself the skills on the job - and quickly moved through various positions to eventually land myself as a full time software engineer at one of the fastest growing tech companies at the time. I definitely got lucky with the organization, but I also put myself in a position to take advantage of that luck.

I leveraged those skills I developed and grew with the company (AdRoll) in San Francisco, moved over to the New York office to try something new, and then when I felt like I was kind of an easy street with that position (well paying, no new challenges). I changed companies to a promising startup (at the time, DataDog) that eventually became one of the most successful IPO's in tech history. Amazing experiences that I feel very lucky to have, but there was still something missing.

I moved on to try launch some software products on my own, I ended up taking some contracting gigs with startups as a product manager - leveraging my technical skill set, but with a new scope of responsibility and therefore a new challenge. In the tech world, I realized my unique differentiator was to be a bridge between technical teams and non-technical business teams.

Throughout these last few years - I had my eye on organizations related to football and have always had a strong interest in finding a way to get involved. With all my experiences, I realized there are all these patterns in every organization I've been involved with - and I had a skill set that can help these organizations scale in an efficient manner. Since I've always been an admirer of football programs and coaches in particular, through my reading of books like The Leadership Secrets of Nick Saban, I believe that these organizations deal with the same challenges as any business and that I had the potential to add value to them.

So, in parallel to my "day job" - I've tried to take up some projects to see if my hypothesis was correct, here are some quick examples:

  • I found a way to interview with multiple staff at the Cal Poly Football program and try to dig into their pain points of their day to day. Quickly found out that the Graduate Assistants spent ~8 hours a week watching film to prepare a spreadsheet that they prepared in a particular way for each coordinator. Learning that, within two days - I built the staff there a one step web app that would create 95% of the spreadsheets with the click of a button (the one exception was a nuance with QB runs and differentiating between a scramble and an intended rushing attempt). A few hours of effort for a software script gave Cal PolyGA's an extra ~7.5 hr allowing them to focus on other things to help make them 1 play better and raise there chances of winning the next game
  • When a childhood friend of mine who played professionally, coached in college decided he was building his own Sports Management Company (VSG Sports) he decided to reach out to me since he knew I advised companies in my industry. I jumped at the opportunity to advise him and helped him build out his business model, raise financing, and executed a few technical projects to help him automate his prospect discovery pipeline. The technical project made the prospect discovery part of his business more of a proactive program integrated with a CRM, instead of a reactive manual process with minimal structure.

I'm at a point in my life where I realize the most important thing I can do for myself is to get an opportunity to work on a fulfilling career that's been a lifelong passion. I strongly feel that I can add real value very quickly. I would be eternally grateful to be able to share my background and ideate where I can contribute to a football org and make them an improved program by leveraging my background. With the AI revolution that's happening, I can evangelize and build custom tools that leverage these new and rapidly evolving technologies - and I have a proven track record of contribution that I'm certain applies to these types of organizations.

If you made it this far, I can't tell you how truly grateful I am for your time. I would welcome an opportunity for a call to tell you more, or even better, fly for a face to face meeting to discuss how I can contribute. Whether it's about optimizing your data pipeline or why the double-wing offense became obselete, I'm game for the conversation. Thank you.
